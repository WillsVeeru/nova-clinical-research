Understanding clinical research

Clear, compassionate answers to the questions patients and families ask most — written for everyone, no medical training required.

01

What is a clinical trial?

A clinical trial is a research study with volunteers that helps doctors learn whether a new medicine, device, or approach is safe and works well.

Each trial follows a written plan, called a protocol, that explains who can join, what happens at each visit, and how information is collected.

02

Why participate in clinical research?

People take part for many reasons — to learn more about their health condition, to receive study-related care and monitoring, or to help future patients.

Every medicine available today was made possible by people who volunteered for research. Participation does not guarantee any personal benefit.

03

Understanding informed consent

Before you join, the research team explains the study in plain language — its purpose, procedures, possible risks and benefits, and your rights.

You will receive a written consent form to review and can take time to ask questions or discuss it with family or your doctor. You only sign if you choose to take part.

04

Are clinical trials safe?

Clinical trials are carefully regulated. Each study is reviewed by an independent ethics committee (Institutional Review Board) that protects participants’ rights and well-being.

The research team monitors your health throughout the study. All research involves some risk, and the team will explain known risks before you decide.

05

What are the possible risks and benefits?

Possible benefits may include close health monitoring and contributing to medical knowledge. The investigational treatment may or may not help you.

Possible risks can include side effects, time commitments for visits and tests, or a treatment that does not work as hoped. Risks differ for every study.

06

Can I withdraw from a study?

Yes. Participation is always voluntary. You may leave a study at any time, for any reason, without penalty or loss of benefits you are otherwise entitled to.

If you decide to withdraw, the research team may ask about follow-up steps that help protect your safety.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

01Who can participate?

Every study has its own eligibility requirements, which may include age, health history, and current condition. The research team reviews these requirements with you before any study activity begins.

02Does participation cost money?

Whether there are costs or payments depends on the individual study and its protocol. The research team will explain the details for a specific study during the informed consent discussion.

03Will I receive the investigational treatment?

This depends on the study design. Some studies compare an investigational approach with a placebo or another treatment, and how participants are assigned is described in the study protocol and consent form.

04Do I need a referral from my doctor?

A referral is generally not required to contact us. With your permission, the research team may coordinate with your physician where appropriate.

05How do I find out if I may qualify?

Call our office at (469) 505-3451 or use the participant inquiry form on the Contact page. Please do not include detailed medical information in the form; the team will discuss it with you privately.

06Is my information private?

Information you submit through the website is used only to respond to your inquiry. Health information collected during a study is protected as described in the study's informed consent document. See our Privacy Policy for details.
